# Functional Requirements

Site Operations covers day-to-day operational workflows and coordination. Estate Management covers strategic performance, investment and long-term planning. Both areas are listed where a requirement directly supports both responsibilities.

| Area of application | Requirement | Functional requirement description | Data required | Data output | Technology choices and AI opportunities | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Visitors | FR-01 | Sell tickets and family passes and validate admission. | Ticket products, prices, party details, payments and entitlements. | Tickets, payment confirmations and admission records. | Purchased ticketing platform, payment gateway and QR/RFID readers; deterministic validation. | Family passes are explicit in the brief; pass media and offline-entry rules remain open. |
| Visitors | FR-02 | Provide attraction information and guidance reflecting current estate conditions. | Maps, opening status, queues, accessibility, preferences and animal-visibility observations. | Relevant attraction suggestions, routes and visitor information. | Mobile/web app with cached content; AI could combine animal visibility, crowd levels and visitor interests to recommend suitable alternatives. | Animal visibility and offline content came from team discussion; stale information must be identified. |
| Visitors | FR-03 | Collect and analyse feedback to understand visitor satisfaction and preferences. | Surveys, comments, ratings and relevant visit context. | Feedback themes, recurring issues and improvement opportunities. | Feedback tools and analytics; language models could group multilingual comments and relate themes to operational problems. | Separate reported opinions from measured facts and avoid treating sentiment as proof of causation. |
| Visitors | FR-04 | Manage consent-based communications, offers and referrals that encourage repeat visits. | Customer/pass records, consent, visit history, preferences and campaign responses. | Relevant communications, offers, referrals and repeat-visit measures. | Purchased CRM and campaign tools; recommendation models and controlled experiments. | Pete requested a CRM buy-versus-build decision; Salesforce was an example, not a selection. |
| Animals | FR-05 | Maintain animal and group records covering health, care and observations. | Animal/cohort identity, keeper observations, veterinary records and care events. | Current care histories, follow-up tasks and authorised summaries. | Animal-management platform, structured records and role-based access. | Detailed clinical records should not be exposed to visitor-facing services. |
| Animals | FR-06 | Schedule feeding and record consumption and feeding behaviour. | Diet plans, feeding schedules, food offered, leftovers and observations. | Feeding records, missed-feed alerts and consumption trends. | Care workflows and mobile recording; vision or time-series analysis could flag changes in feeding behaviour. | The brief asks about feeding; abnormal behaviour requires keeper interpretation. |
| Animals | FR-07 | Monitor animal and enclosure conditions and raise welfare concerns. | Environmental readings, health observations, behaviour and species-specific limits. | Local alerts, welfare trends and keeper/vet tasks. | Edge threshold alarms with buffered telemetry; AI could combine behavioural and environmental changes to suggest early investigation. | Immediate response must not depend on cloud connectivity or AI inference. |
| Animals | FR-08 | Monitor jumping piranha population trends and flag concerning changes. | Verified observations, sampled images/video, previous estimates and observation conditions. | Population estimates, uncertainty ranges, trends and investigation tasks. | Manual sampling baseline; computer vision could assist estimation across repeated observations. | The brief specifies population trends, not exact automated counts; Helen highlights occlusion, double counting and validation gaps. |
| Rides | FR-09 | Maintain authoritative ride, inspection, defect and operating-status records. | Asset details, inspections, defects, status changes and service history. | Current ride records, outstanding issues and an audit trail. | Asset-management or maintenance platform with relational storage and APIs. | Core record keeping needs reliable workflows rather than AI. |
| Rides | FR-10 | Schedule and track maintenance through engineer-approved return to service. | Inspection schedules, defects, work orders, resources and completion evidence. | Maintenance plans, assignments, completed work and approved status changes. | Maintenance workflow engine; optimisation could suggest schedules using demand, skills and parts availability. | Engineers retain approval authority; safety obligations take precedence over demand. |
| Rides | FR-11 | Detect ride-condition changes requiring investigation or maintenance. | Vibration, temperature, current, runtime, faults and maintenance outcomes. | Condition alerts, supporting evidence and investigation recommendations. | MQTT sensors, edge buffering and time-series storage; anomaly detection could identify developing faults before fixed thresholds are crossed. | Compare against threshold alerts; remaining-life estimates require sufficient fault history. |
| Rides | FR-12 | Help engineers find relevant maintenance instructions and historical evidence. | Approved manuals, inspection reports, service history and access permissions. | Cited guidance and relevant previous maintenance cases. | Search with retrieval-augmented generation over approved documents. | Proposed in Matt's document; answers support qualified engineers and cannot authorise work. |
| Site Operations | FR-13 | Estimate queues and help staff and visitors respond to congestion. | Arrivals, departures, queue counts, throughput and ride status. | Queue estimates, confidence levels, congestion alerts and alternative suggestions. | Edge counters and rules-based estimates; ML could forecast congestion and assess the effect of redirecting visitors. | Helen requests clearer accuracy and freshness criteria; suggestions must respect welfare and capacity limits. |
| Site Operations | FR-14 | Manage incidents, staff tasks, closures and operational-status updates. | Staff reports, sensor alerts, attraction status, locations and task progress. | Assigned tasks, incident records, approved closures and public updates. | Workflow platform, staff app and domain events; AI could summarise incidents and suggest priorities. | Emergency instructions and closure authority remain with authorised staff. |
| Estate Management | FR-15 | Report on estate performance to support staffing budgets, maintenance investment and long-term planning. | Attendance, revenue, costs, popularity, downtime, care activity and repeat visits. | Strategic performance dashboards, investment evidence and long-term demand forecasts. | BI platform and analytical data store; forecasting and scenario analysis could compare investment and capacity options against expected demand. | Estate Management consumes domain records for strategic decisions; day-to-day task allocation remains with Site Operations and domain teams. Include ongoing AI costs. |
| Site Operations; Estate Management | FR-16 | Measure the popularity of rides, exhibits and estate areas. | Anonymous counts, throughput, operating hours and closures. | Usage trends, popularity comparisons and demand indicators. | Counters, MQTT telemetry and analytics; forecasting could distinguish seasonal patterns from unusual demand. | Site Operations owns usage observations; Estate Management uses trends for investment and capacity planning. Account for closures and capacity limits. |
