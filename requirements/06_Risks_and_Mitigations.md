# Risks and Mitigations

Estate-wide risks covering Visitor Journey and Growth, Crowd and Site Operations, Ride Operations, Animal Care and Welfare, and the shared platform. Controls described in the architecture are proposed, not implemented assurances; numerical acceptance targets remain subject to review and validation.

Risk areas follow the areas of application in Functional_Requirements.md and identify the functions directly affected, rather than general oversight or downstream consequences. “All” denotes risks affecting all four areas.

| Risk Area | Risk Sub-Area | Description | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visitors | Offline Admission and Payments | Signed cached entitlements support disconnected entry but do not establish current revocation or prevent reuse across isolated gates. Payment retries and delayed confirmation can also produce inconsistent orders and passes. | Agree the proposed four-hour entry window and accepted fraud exposure. Define local duplicate-use checks, expiry, revocation refresh and staff exceptions. Use idempotent payment and entitlement operations; rehearse purchase, disconnected entry and reconciliation, including refunds and conflicting gate records. |
| Visitors | Inclusive Visitor Experience | An app-centred journey can exclude visitors without smartphones, connectivity or suitable accessibility support. Cached content may remain useful while cached operational guidance becomes misleading. | Retain paper admission, signage and staff assistance. Test purchasing and guidance with representative visitors and assistive technologies. Separate downloadable reference content from expiring live status; show update times and provide a clear fallback when routes or attraction availability cannot be confirmed. |
| Visitors | Procurement and Integration | Buying CRM and other commodity capabilities may accelerate delivery, but vendor APIs, licensing, offline support and export limits could conflict with domain ownership. The requested CRM buy-versus-build decision remains to be completed; Salesforce is only a candidate. | Compare total cost, functional fit and integration constraints before selection. Prove ticketing, consent, event publication and data export through a small integration trial. Record the CRM decision, authoritative fields and exit route; avoid customising purchased products into a second estate platform. |
| Animals | Piranha Population Estimation | The newer draft combines PIT detections, vision and manual counts, but existing tags, detection coverage and estimator assumptions are unverified. A later count cannot label every earlier estimate if the population changed; eight quarterly counts provide limited calibration evidence. | Run a keeper-approved feasibility trial covering tag coverage, occlusion, detection bias and counting disturbance. Record births, deaths and transfers; compare estimates with time-matched, independently checked observations. Report intervals and limitations, validate change-detection performance, and retain human approval of recorded population updates. |
| Animals | Animal Record Authority | The original capability makes Animal Care authoritative, while the newer draft proposes reconciliation with ZIMS. Existing ZIMS use, accreditation and specific obligations are not established by the brief, creating a risk of conflicting records and unjustified integration assumptions. | Confirm the actual record system and applicable obligations before selecting an integration. Define field ownership, authorised amendments, synchronisation and conflict handling. Keep operational estimates distinct from verified stock records, with named approval and auditable reconciliation where an external record is used. |
| Animals | Enclosure Equipment and Sensor Quality | The condition and monitoring capability of existing enclosure equipment are unverified. Water, humidity, fouling, animal interference or poor sensor placement could produce misleading environmental and feeding observations. | Survey equipment and sensing conditions with keepers and relevant specialists. Select suitable devices, validate placement and compare readings with manual checks. Establish cleaning, calibration and replacement schedules; flag unreliable readings and retain direct care observations when monitoring fails. |
| Animals | Animal Welfare Boundaries | The design assumes independent local welfare alarms and life-support controls, but their coverage and response arrangements remain unverified. A locally raised alarm does not ensure timely care; missed escalation or inappropriate AI advice could harm animals. | Have keepers and veterinary specialists define species-specific limits, response times and escalation responsibilities. Verify local protection during gateway, network and cloud failures. Retain scheduled feeding and welfare checks, require qualified approval for care changes, and exercise alarm acknowledgement and manual intervention. |
| Rides | Historic Ride Equipment and Sensor Quality | Historic rides may be difficult to instrument without affecting their operation or existing protection. Poor sensor placement, calibration drift or missed readings could give misleading condition and maintenance evidence. | Survey each ride with qualified engineers and pilot non-invasive sensing. Verify that installation does not interfere with protective controls; calibrate readings against inspections and known operating conditions. Flag suspect inputs and retain scheduled inspections where telemetry is unreliable or unavailable. |
| Rides | Ride Safety Boundaries | The brief describes inspected rides, but the design assumes local protective controls whose capabilities still need verification. Integration errors or reliance on AI maintenance advice could weaken protections for riders and staff or permit an unauthorised return to service. | Have qualified ride engineers confirm protective controls and approval boundaries. Keep safety functions independent of gateways, cloud services and AI; require completed inspections and authorised return to service. Rehearse dependency failures and attempted approval bypasses in a safe test environment. |
| Site Operations and Estate Management | Financial Sustainability and Scope | The loss-making estate must fund hardware, integration, support and AI before benefits are proven. Additional digital-twin, agent and optimisation proposals could delay essential capabilities and increase recurring costs. | Deliver ticketing, operational records and priority sensing first. Cost each phase, including installation, staffing, calibration and replacement. Gate expansion on measured value; assess additional AI proposals individually against simpler alternatives and stop pilots that do not justify their cost. |
| Visitors, Site Operations and Estate Management | Privacy and Visitor Tracking | Proposed RFID passes, household accounts, cameras and personalisation could turn anonymous popularity measurement into identifiable movement histories. Cached consent and queued campaigns can outlive a withdrawal. | Separate admission identity from crowd counts. Document collection purposes, minimise retained data and restrict household and clinical access. Enforce current communication permission at dispatch, including queued campaigns; test withdrawal, deletion and public-data contracts before extending tracking or AI features. |
| Visitors, Site Operations and Estate Management | Benefits and Decision Quality | Popularity can reflect closures, capacity, visibility or sensor coverage rather than preference. More attendance or recommendation clicks need not improve profitability, repeat visits or welfare. | Combine usage with availability, costs and observation quality. Establish comparable seasonal baselines and controlled growth experiments. Measure contribution, satisfaction and repeat visits alongside operational guardrails; keep unproven benefit targets and additional income ideas separate from demonstrated results. |
| Animals, Rides | Unsafe AI Guidance and Tool Use | Cited answers can still misrepresent manuals or care instructions. Retrieved documents and prompts can manipulate assistants; proposed agents would extend the exposure if their tools can change domain records. | Restrict retrieval to authorised, versioned sources and test whether citations support each answer. Enforce permissions and approval gates outside the model. Adversarially test retrieval and tool calls; retain qualified review for consequential advice and provide direct access to approved procedures when AI fails. |
| Visitors, Animals, Site Operations and Estate Management | Animal Visibility and Crowd Guidance | Reader passage does not guarantee that an animal is visible. Stale queue data or simultaneous recommendations can redirect visitors into congestion or disturb animals. The proposed autonomous visibility forecast therefore has consequences beyond a disappointing prediction. | Validate visibility against keeper observations. Preserve UNKNOWN, keeper overrides and restricted publication for sensitive species. Apply current closure, accessibility, welfare and capacity rules after prediction; damp notifications and test suppression across apps and signs. Withdraw expired guidance and suspend forecasts on agreed quality or crowd-impact breaches. |
| Animals, Rides, Site Operations and Estate Management | Connectivity and Edge Failure | Patchy Wi-Fi is an explicit constraint. Proposed store-and-forward handles backhaul loss, but mains-powered gateway failure can also remove a zone's collection and local alerting. Cellular coverage is unconfirmed. | Survey radio coverage, power and installation conditions. Provide proportionate backup power, spare gateways and independent critical alarm paths. Test link loss, full buffers, power loss and gateway replacement separately, including local staff procedures and the proposed 24-hour buffering window. |
| All | Growth and Capacity | Tripling daily attendance does not define peak arrivals, concurrent purchases, sensor traffic or physical capacity. Unsized gateways and central services could fail when queues and staff workload are highest. | Build a workload model covering all 40 rides and 55 enclosures, device rates, payloads, peak admissions and simultaneous backlog replay. Size buffers and backhaul explicitly; load-test the proposed NFR profile. Couple digital growth plans to safe site capacity, staffing and attraction throughput. |
| All | Event Consistency and Replay | At-least-once delivery and independent domain records introduce duplicates, ordering gaps and stale projections. Replaying historical observations could create current alerts, tasks or misleading attraction status. | Implement the proposed outbox, event identifiers, aggregate ordering and versioned contracts. Enforce event-time and freshness checks at action boundaries. Reconcile offline conflicts through domain rules, quarantine exceptions and test replay alongside live traffic without duplicate business effects. |
| All | Central Recovery and Shared Dependencies | Domain separation does not remove common dependencies on identity, the event platform and central storage. Backups alone cannot establish consistent recovery of orders, care records and event positions. | Map shared failure points and define degraded behaviour for each domain. Rehearse restoring records, credentials and event positions together against the proposed recovery targets; reconcile payment and offline records afterwards. Preserve local critical functions and pause analytics when necessary. |
| All | AI Evidence, Cold Start and Drift | Paper-based operations provide little dependable training or outcome data. Proposed maintenance lead times, welfare detection and growth improvements may not hold across ride types, species or seasons; synthetic tests cannot establish field performance. | Establish non-AI baselines and capture verified outcomes first. Evaluate by supported operating condition, including missed events and false-alert workload. Use shadow trials, explicit promotion thresholds and ongoing outcome review; abstain or revert to rules when evidence is insufficient. |
| All | Provider Dependency and AI Cost | Provider-neutral interfaces help replacement but do not make models behaviourally interchangeable. Price rises, shutdowns or licensing changes can invalidate the economics and evaluation of a use case. | Retain evaluation data, prompts and contracts independently. Set per-capability budgets and enforce ceilings on discretionary inference. Rehearse price spikes and provider loss using tested non-AI fallbacks; evaluate any replacement before activation and record migration effort and contractual exit limits. |
| All | Device and Platform Compromise | Exposed sensors, gateways and shared services create routes for forged telemetry, stolen credentials and ransomware. The proposed identity and access controls require ongoing operation across intermittently connected equipment. | Segment visitor, device and operational networks; enforce device identities, topic permissions and least privilege. Inventory devices and owners, rotate credentials, rate-limit ingestion and stage authenticated updates where supported. Exercise revocation, compromise isolation and recovery without disabling independent local protection. |
| All | Staff Adoption and Operational Ownership | The brief provides hiring budget but no existing IT team. Four domains, edge devices and several model types create a substantial support burden; poor adoption or excessive alerts could leave records incomplete and incidents unattended. | Name business and technical owners, fund field support and co-design workflows with staff. Reconcile priority paper records, pilot offline task recording and train through realistic incidents. Monitor missed care, unresolved defects, sync failures and alert workload before expanding the platform. |
| All | Design and Acceptance Consistency | Integrated drafts, capability appendices and newer NFRs differ on animal-record ownership, autonomy and numerical thresholds. Unreconciled versions could present assumptions as constraints or imply that proposed controls have passed testing. | Agree one submission baseline and reconcile affected ADRs, diagrams and requirements. Mark decisions and targets by approval status, assign acceptance owners and retain test evidence. Resolve critical open thresholds before release, with explicit residual-risk acceptance for remaining gaps. |
